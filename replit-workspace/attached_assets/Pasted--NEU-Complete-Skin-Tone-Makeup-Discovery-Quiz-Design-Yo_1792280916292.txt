# NEU: Complete Skin Tone & Makeup Discovery Quiz Design

## Your task

Design and build the complete interactive frontend experience for Neu's skin-tone and makeup discovery quiz.

**This is a UI/UX design task only.**

Do not build backend integrations, databases, authentication, recommendation algorithms, APIs, or account systems. Use illustrative data clearly labeled as examples.

The quiz must feel like a personalized beauty consultation, not a clinical assessment, personality test, or lengthy questionnaire.

Users should leave feeling that Neu understands their skin and can help them discover makeup worth exploring.

## 1. Design direction

Follow Neu's existing approved website aesthetic.

**Visual identity**

- Main background: `#FBF7F2`
- Soft secondary background: `#F3ECEB`
- Primary button: `#514659`
- Main text: `#322B34`
- Accent: `#786B88`
- Secondary accent: `#B85F78`
- Headlines: Cormorant Garamond
- Body: DM Sans
- Utility labels: Space Mono, used sparingly

**Design principles**

- Minimal, sophisticated, welcoming and editorial.
- Lots of intentional whitespace.
- One primary question per screen.
- Large, readable headlines.
- Accessible color contrast.
- Subtle transitions.
- No heavy shadows or floating cards.
- No human faces or photographs.
- No excessive icons, gradients, or decorative illustrations.
- No unnecessary information panels.
- No horizontal overflow on mobile.
- Never use tiny or faded instructional text.
- Do not use em dashes or emojis.

The quiz should visually belong to the existing Neu homepage.

## 2. Overall quiz layout

Use a consistent layout throughout.

**Top navigation**

- Small Neu wordmark on the left.
- Thin progress indicator.
- Close button on the right.

**Main content**

- Centered question.
- One or two sentences of helpful explanation.
- Beautiful, spacious selection interface.
- Contextual "Need help?" information where relevant.

**Bottom navigation**

- Back
- Continue
- Skip, when appropriate

On mobile, keep navigation easy to reach without covering content.

Answers should remain selected when navigating backward.

Create a smooth, clickable frontend prototype.

Do not require users to create an account to explore the quiz.

## 3. Screen 1: Welcome

**Headline: Let's get to know your skin.**

Supporting text:

Tell us a little about your skin, the makeup you already love, and what you're looking for. We'll help you discover products worth trying.

Primary button:

**Find my match**

Below the button:

No camera needed. No makeup expertise required.

Keep this screen extremely minimal.

## 4. Screen 2: Current makeup

**Headline: What complexion makeup do you currently use?**

Supporting text:

Your current products can help us understand what shades have worked for you.

Provide options:

- Foundation
- Concealer
- Skin tint
- BB or CC cream
- I don't use complexion makeup
- I'm new to makeup

If the user selects a product type, display:

- Search brand
- Search product
- Select exact shade

Show recognizable brand names, including drugstore and prestige brands.

Examples include Maybelline, L'Oréal, NYX, e.l.f., Milani, Revlon, CoverGirl, Fenty Beauty, NARS, MAC, Rare Beauty, Huda Beauty, Estée Lauder and Clinique.

Use example content only where verified catalog information is unavailable. Do not invent shade names and present them as genuine products.

Allow users to add more than one product.

Provide "I don't know my shade" and Skip options.

## 5. Screen 3: How well does your makeup match?

**Headline: How does your current shade look on you?**

Supporting text:

There are no wrong answers. Tell us what you've noticed.

Design large, clean selectable choices:

- It blends in perfectly
- A little too light
- A little too dark
- Too orange
- Too pink
- Too yellow
- Gray or ashy
- Changes color after application
- I'm not sure

Allow multiple selections when appropriate, but keep contradictory choices understandable.

If the user entered more than one product, show this screen for each relevant product without overwhelming them.

## 6. Screen 4: Inclusive skin-depth selection

**Headline: Which shade looks closest to your skin?**

Supporting text:

Choose your closest starting shade. You can always change it later.

### The palette is extremely important

Create an inclusive visual skin-depth palette representing people from the very fairest through the very deepest complexions.

Use **40 thoughtfully distributed starting shades**.

Ensure meaningful visual differences across the full spectrum.

Do not represent fair skin with 15 choices and deep skin with only three.

The deepest complexions must receive the same design attention and selection precision as the lightest.

Suggested visual groupings:

1. Very fair
2. Fair
3. Light
4. Light-medium
5. Medium
6. Tan
7. Deep
8. Very deep

Five shades per grouping can serve as an initial design structure, but these categories should not become rigid biological classifications.

### Selection interaction

**A slider is not required.**

Choose the best interaction design.

Preferred direction:

A beautiful, responsive grid of individual skin-tone swatches, organized into clearly labeled depth groups.

When someone selects a swatch:

- Highlight it elegantly.
- Show a larger selected-color preview.
- Display an accessible shade label.
- Offer nearby shades for easy comparison.
- Allow users to change selections freely.

If useful, add a "Show nearby shades" interaction or a zoomed comparison view.

Do not introduce a slider unless it genuinely improves usability.

### Important inclusivity requirements

- Include very fair through very deep complexions.
- Ensure fair, medium, tan, deep and very deep shades are meaningfully represented.
- Do not assume warm, cool, neutral or olive characteristics from shade depth alone.
- Do not represent olive simply by making a depth swatch green.
- Ensure every selectable swatch has an accessible text label.
- Don't rely solely on color to indicate selection.
- Support keyboard navigation.
- Include an "I'm not sure" option.
- Include an option to use an existing foundation shade as a starting reference.

Use the existing Neu shade palette where appropriate, refining the interface for inclusivity.

Digital colors are approximations. Do not claim that an on-screen swatch identifies someone's exact real-world complexion.

Add a short note:

"Screen colors and lighting can affect how shades appear. This is just a starting point."

## 7. Screen 5: Undertone education

**Headline: Let's understand your undertone.**

Supporting text:

Your skin depth tells us how light or deep your skin is. Your undertone helps explain why certain makeup shades may look different on you.

Create a minimal, educational visual explanation of:

**Warm**

Golden, yellow or peach characteristics.

**Cool**

Pink, rosy or sometimes bluish characteristics.

**Neutral**

A balance of warm and cool characteristics.

**Olive**

A muted greenish or gray-green quality that can appear alongside warm, cool or neutral undertones.

Make it clear that olive is not limited to medium or tan skin.

All undertone characteristics can appear across a broad range of skin depths.

Use elegant color examples, simple typography and plenty of whitespace.

Provide a Continue button.

## 8. Screen 6: Wrist vein guide

**Headline: What color do your wrist veins look?**

Supporting text:

This can give us a clue about your undertone, but it won't determine it on its own.

Include an expandable section titled:

**How to check your wrist veins**

Explain clearly:

1. Stand near a window in indirect natural daylight.
2. Turn your wrist so your palm faces upward.
3. Relax your hand.
4. Look at the thin lines beneath the skin on your inner wrist or forearm.
5. Choose the appearance closest to what you see.

Use minimal, understandable visual illustrations of vein colors on different skin depths.

Do not use human-face photography.

Options:

- Mostly blue or purple
- Mostly green or teal
- Both blue and green
- I can't see them clearly
- I'm not sure

Provide a small explanation of the traditional associations:

Blue or purple is sometimes associated with cool undertones.

Green or teal is sometimes associated with warm undertones.

Mixed colors are sometimes associated with neutral undertones.

**Important:** Vein color is not a scientifically reliable standalone undertone test.

Do not treat green veins as proof of olive skin.

Users who cannot see their veins must feel equally included and should be able to continue without difficulty.

## 9. Screen 7: Additional undertone clues

**Headline: What have you noticed about your skin?**

Supporting text:

Sometimes your makeup tells you more than your veins do.

Provide clean selectable answers:

- My skin looks more golden or peachy
- My skin looks more rosy or pink
- My skin looks fairly balanced
- My skin sometimes looks muted or gray-green
- I'm not sure

Add a subtle "Need help understanding these?" expandable explanation.

Do not require users to identify their own undertone.

Avoid asking the same makeup-mismatch question again if it was already answered earlier.

## 10. Screen 8: Olive characteristics

**Headline: Does your skin have a slightly muted or greenish cast?**

Supporting text:

Some people notice that their skin has a subtle green-gray quality, even when they're fair, medium or deep.

Options:

- Yes, I've noticed this
- Maybe
- No
- I'm not sure

Include a "What does olive mean?" explanation.

Explain that warm olive, neutral olive and cool olive are possible.

Do not visually associate olive with only one depth range.

Do not force users to identify as olive.

## 13. Screen 11: Your profile preview

**Headline: Your skin, a little more understood.**

Show an elegant, editable skin-profile summary.

Include:

- Selected skin-depth swatch
- Tentative undertone
- Olive characteristics, if selected
- Current makeup products
- Makeup categories of interest
- Optional budget and finish preferences

Show a short section:

**What brought us here**

Explain which answers contributed to the example profile.

Never present a definite undertone result based only on wrist veins.

When information is uncertain, use "Still exploring" or "Possibly."

Make it clear that this is a design prototype and results are illustrative.

Primary action:

**Discover makeup for my skin**

Secondary action:

**Edit my answers**

## 14. Final discovery preview

Show a minimalist preview of what the user will eventually discover.

Include clearly labeled sample product recommendations showing:

- Brand
- Product name
- Shade
- Category
- Brief reason it may be relevant

Show a small "Products loved by people with skin like yours" preview using clearly labeled illustrative content.

Do not invent genuine user reviews, ratings or matches.

The purpose is to demonstrate the intended product experience visually.

## 15. Interaction and usability

Build the full quiz as a clickable frontend prototype.

All screens must connect correctly.

Users must be able to:

- Start the quiz
- Select or deselect answers
- Navigate backward
- Skip optional questions
- Expand educational explanations
- Explore all skin-depth swatches
- Compare nearby shades
- Change their selected shade
- Select multiple makeup categories
- Review their choices
- Edit previous answers
- Reach the final preview

Preserve selections during navigation.

Use realistic interaction states, including validation and responsive layout.

Do not require account creation.

## 16. Accessibility and inclusivity

This must be a priority throughout the design.

- Support the full skin-depth spectrum.
- Give equal visual importance to very fair and very deep skin.
- Keep skin depth separate from undertone.
- Represent olive characteristics across skin depths.
- Never assume someone's race or ethnicity from their shade.
- Allow uncertainty and skipped answers.
- Use large enough text and touch targets.
- Meet appropriate WCAG AA contrast requirements.
- Make all controls keyboard-accessible.
- Provide visible focus states.
- Use text labels in addition to colors.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Test across mobile, tablet and desktop.
- Avoid presenting subjective undertone clues as scientific diagnoses.

## 17. What not to build

Do not:

- Redesign the homepage.
- Build Supabase integrations.
- Build authentication or two-factor authentication.
- Modify database schemas.
- Build a recommendation engine.
- Implement image uploads.
- Add camera or face scanning.
- Build shopping checkout.
- Generate fake reviews.
- Invent real community statistics.
- Add excessive dashboard cards.
- Add unnecessary animations.
- Use AI-generated human faces.
- Use a slider simply because it is conventional.
- Deploy or push changes without approval.

## 18. Final deliverable

Build a polished, fully clickable, responsive quiz design that follows the approved Neu brand identity.

The flow should feel natural:

**Current makeup → Makeup experience → Skin depth → Undertone education → Wrist veins → Additional clues → Olive characteristics → Makeup interests → Preferences → Skin profile → Discovery preview**

Keep the experience adaptive. Skip irrelevant screens when the user has already provided the necessary information.

The quiz should be enjoyable for someone who understands makeup and equally approachable for someone who has never purchased foundation.

Most importantly, **no one should feel that their complexion, undertone, makeup experience or level of knowledge has been overlooked.**

Present the completed frontend prototype for visual approval before implementing backend functionality.

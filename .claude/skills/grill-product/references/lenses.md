# Lenses

Question banks for grilling a product decision. Pull from the lenses that fit; do not run all five by default. For named frameworks (MOAT, motions, value metric, shape, tradeoff axes), see [frameworks.md](frameworks.md).

The leading questions behind every lens: **Why** does this deserve to exist, **What** are we building to solve it, **How** should users experience it, **When** is the right moment to build, launch, price, or scale. AI changed the tools, not these.

Boundary between the first two lenses: **Signal Reading** turns what was said into the real problem. **Framing** decides whether that problem deserves a product and what the product is. Read the signal first, then frame.

---

## Lens 1 — Signal Reading

Users give signals, not answers. We are not our users — many product mistakes start by building from our own context, roadmap pressure, and technical excitement instead of the user's reality.

- What type of signal is this, and how strong? Classify it before acting (see frameworks.md, Signal Taxonomy). What is the underlying need behind it?
- Is this the real problem, or a symptom in the user's own language? Are you about to build a "faster horse" — the solution they could name rather than the one they need?
- Did the user describe a symptom, ask for a familiar solution, or reveal a workaround? What does each actually point to?
- Whose reality is this built from — ours or theirs? Which of our assumptions is doing the heavy lifting? Which bias is at work — confirmation, survivorship, internal, premature convergence?
- Who benefits when this problem keeps existing? (Surfaces resistance and status-quo gravity.)
- What behavior (not stated opinion) supports this read? What evidence would change it?

Output: the interpreted problem, separated from what was literally said.

---

## Lens 2 — Framing

Great products make users more capable: they solve a real problem, reduce friction, make the next step clearer, and work reliably enough to be trusted. A product is not great because it is clever.

- **Why** does this deserve to exist? What real problem does it solve, and for whom?
- Vitamin or painkiller? If a vitamin, what would have to change to make it a painkiller — and is that worth doing?
- **What** exactly are we building to solve it? What capability does the user gain?
- **How** should users experience it? What does "reliable enough to be trusted" mean here?
- **When** is the right moment to build, launch, price, or scale?
- Who is it really for — which segment, in which reality? (see frameworks.md, Name The Segment)
- Does it make the user more capable, or is it clever for its own sake?

---

## Lens 3 — Tradeoffs & Shape

Strategy is the tradeoffs you will defend — not the list of everything you want to build. Remove enough ambiguity to give the thing shape.

- Which tradeoff axes apply, and what is the defended choice on each? (see frameworks.md, Tradeoff Axes)
- How do competitors solve this, and which choices are table stakes vs a real differentiator? (see frameworks.md, Competitor Landscape) You cannot judge whether a tradeoff is defensible without the landscape.
- Clear enough to build: What should happen? What should never happen? What does success look like? What is the quality bar? What is in and out of scope? What must be true before launch?
- Product shape: should it be Guided, Suggested, or Open — and why? This is product strategy, not a UX detail, and it applies to AI products too. (see frameworks.md, Product Shape)

---

## Lens 4 — GTM & Pricing

A product is how users discover, understand, experience, trust, and decide to pay for value. Product is part of go-to-market.

- Which acquisition model fits the market — run the MOAT framework. (see frameworks.md, MOAT)
- What is the selling motion — bottom-up or top-down — and does the model match it? They repel each other when they don't. (see frameworks.md, Selling Motion)
- Acquisition model: free trial, freemium, or demo — does it fit the market strategy?
- Pricing: what is the value metric, and does it pass the three tests? (see frameworks.md, Value-Metric Pricing)

---

## Lens 5 — First Experience

The first experience is the product — it is where users decide if the promise is real. It is not about teaching every feature; it is about reaching value quickly, clearly, and confidently. This is where product, GTM, and trust meet.

- What is the "aha" — the first moment the user reaches real value?
- How fast can they reach it, and what currently stands in the way?
- What can be cut from onboarding so value comes sooner?
- How does the first run prove the promise is real, clearly enough to trust?
- For whom does the first experience need to work first?

---
name: Neu web MVP scope
description: Owner-adopted product scope and approval boundaries from the web MVP specification.
---

Launch is website-first for US shoppers. Shopping categories are only foundation, concealer, blush and lips, with deliberate support across complexion depths, olive/muted undertones and pigmented or two-toned lips.

**Why:** The owner supplied the web MVP specification and explicitly instructed us to follow it while preserving the existing Neu design system and functionality.

**How to apply:** Preserve existing work. Do not pad the launch with skincare, eye makeup or other functioning shopping categories. Keep prior makeup history distinct from shopping-category scope.

Product facts, exact shades, imagery rights, prices, stock and community evidence must be source-backed and honestly bounded. No fabricated products/reviews/users, uncalibrated fit percentages, paid rankings or unsupported safety promises. Pantone references require proper licensing; do not copy its proprietary swatch library or claim certification.

**Why:** Honesty and auditable evidence are explicit product requirements, not optional polish.

**How to apply:** Historical product observations do not prove current availability or shade suitability. Unknown information stays unknown. Separate complexion depth, undertone, saturation, skin type and lip pigmentation; do not infer ethnicity or diagnosis.

The launch quiz is camera-free and usable anonymously. Selfies, photo storage and face inference are future opt-in work, not MVP scope. Sharing is voluntary, private data must remain private, and reviews must be authentic and non-incentivized.

**Why:** The owner specified a privacy-conscious manual discovery experience and genuine community evidence.

**How to apply:** Use local-only preview paths with clear labels until backend behavior is actually implemented and verified. Do not treat a local draft or moderation simulation as real publication.

Work advances through owner-reviewed stages. The owner accepted Stage 1 based on the report and placed normalized catalog storage, initial explainable matching and two-path onboarding together in Stage 2. Stop after showing the Stage 2 preview, actual tests, verified coverage and gaps.

**Why:** The owner's newer roadmap and onboarding instructions supersede the original separation of catalog and ranking stages.

**How to apply:** Use a feature branch, preserve native/static parity and waitlist wiring, and do not deploy, change production integrations or spend money without separate approval.

The eventual US-market catalog target is 25 selected brands, 100–150 product lines and approximately 2,000–4,000 shades/color variants. Each selected line should eventually contain its complete current US range where reliably verified. These are progressive targets, not Stage 2 exit requirements.

**Why:** The owner explicitly defined these targets and asked for representative verified coverage across drugstore, mid-range and luxury before completing the full catalog.

**How to apply:** Separate verified coverage from data gaps. Partial listings are not complete ranges; estimates and user reports must not become verified product facts.

Onboarding begins with equally prominent known-shade and help-finding-shade paths, producing one editable profile. Keep shared questions essential and move saved category preferences into discovery. Public community stays out of the MVP until 500+ registered users and approval of that phase.

**Why:** The owner supplied a new onboarding brief defining both paths and the community gate.

**How to apply:** Preserve reference inputs even when catalog evidence is missing; no invented equivalences, percentages or confidence scores. Preserve private drafts without implying publication.
